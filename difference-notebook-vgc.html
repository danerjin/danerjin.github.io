HOW TO USE THE DIFFERENCE SHEET
1

Team Preview — Fill in Your Opponent's 6

The moment you see your opponent's team, type each Pokémon into the six slots. Add the held item on the dotted line below each name if you can identify it from the archetype. Speed matters — use abbreviations (Gard, Urshi, Lando-T).

2

Analyze the Matchup Before the Timer Runs Out

Fill the three analysis boxes during team preview. Their Gameplan: TR? Weather? Speed control? Threats to Me: which 1–2 mons blow up my team unchecked? My Win Condition: which of my Pokémon and which sequence wins this match?

3

Damage Calcs — Write Only What Matters

Two or three calcs that will actually affect your decisions. Focus on KO thresholds: "does X KO Y at +1?" Note whether it only works after chip, or under weather. These are your in-battle anchors.

4

Each Game: Bring 4, Then Lead 2

Fill the Bring (4) boxes with your selected four. It's easiest to eliminate the two worst Pokémon in the matchup first. Then lock your Lead (2) (orange boxes). Use the notes area to jot key reads, revealed moves, or surprise sets mid-game. Between games, adjust Bring and Lead based on what you now know.

5

Post-Match Review — This Is Where You Improve

What Did I Learn? captures new info about their team. Misplays is honest self-critique. Rate Decision Quality separately from the result — you can lose well and win sloppily. Track your energy level; patterns across sheets reveal when your focus drops in long tournament days.

The core habit: Use team preview time to fill sections 1 and 2 — not to deliberate. Your thinking should happen before the tournament, in prep. The sheet captures conclusions, not deliberation. In a best-of-3, your opponent is adapting to you. The player who adapts faster wins.
